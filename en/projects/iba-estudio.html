Why I made it

When IBA became my studio, it needed a home of its own, separate from my personal portfolio. The first version presented a development, AI and automation studio. In November 2026 IBA changed focus and became a games and animation studio for brands, and the website was rebuilt to tell that story.

The idea

Show what a brand gains from a game or a character before talking about technology. The site has two services: games for brands, which customers open from a link or QR code and which end with a coupon or WhatsApp, and cartoon animation, with a mascot, Reels and stickers. IBA Games works as proof: games made by the studio itself, which anyone can play.

Each service has its own page, with who it is for, what is included, how it works and the timeline. And a project follows four written steps: briefing, proposal, production and delivery.

Creative process

The hardest part was still the copy. Nobody looks for "a Phaser advergame"; people want their Black Friday post to get noticed. So the site talks about common situations, never made-up clients, and timelines and scope come from the studio's catalog.

Visually I followed IBA's identity, with Nó the octopus as mascot, and used Framer Motion to reveal content calmly as people scroll. All the copy lives in a single file read by every page, so the same text never drifts between pages.

Decisions I made

  • React + TailwindAn SPA that is easy to grow, with components reused across the service pages.
  • Nothing made upNo vanity numbers or fake case studies. The site promises what the studio delivers.
  • Playable proofThe studio's own games are one click away, instead of just describing the service.
  • WhatsApp as the channelThe conversation starts where the client already is, and continues in the same place after delivery.

What I took away

Building my own business's website forced me to define the business, twice. Rebuilding it when IBA changed focus meant deciding again what the studio does and what it no longer does.
